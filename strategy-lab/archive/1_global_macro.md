# 1. Global Macro

**Result (12 Apr – 14 May 2026): +23.0%**

## Thesis

Position directionally around the dominant macro catalyst of the window: an
active Iran/Strait of Hormuz conflict disrupting oil supply, pushing gold and
defense equities higher, while equity indices stayed under pressure from the
combination of war-driven inflation and a CAPE ratio near 40x (second-highest
level on record).

## Key positions

| Position | Direction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Gold miners (GDX / GDXJ) | Long | War premium + Hormuz closure = structural gold demand |
| Brent crude futures | Long | Hormuz blockage restricts ~17M barrels/day of transit capacity |
| US defense (LMT / RTX / NOC) | Long | Active war = structural defense spending cycle |
| S&P 500 mini futures | Short | Death cross + CAPE ~40x + war-driven inflation = poor risk/reward for equities |

## What happened

Gold miners (GDX/GDXJ) rallied +53% over the window as the Hormuz closure
persisted longer than initial ceasefire talks suggested, and as inflation
expectations pushed real-asset demand higher. That single position generated
nearly all of the strategy's alpha. Brent and defense equities contributed
modestly (+9.3% and +7.2% respectively). The short S&P position lost ground
(-13.8%) as the index staged an unexpected 14% rally following a ceasefire
and a strong Nvidia earnings report — the inflation/war thesis was directionally
right but the equity short fought against a powerful, unanticipated relief rally.

## Lessons learned

- **Concentration risk was the real driver, not "macro skill."** Remove the
  GDX/GDXJ position and this strategy returns +5.2% — the worst of the six.
  Honest attribution matters more than a flattering headline number.
- **Directional shorts are vulnerable to regime breaks.** The equity short
  was thesis-consistent but lost money the moment sentiment flipped on a
  ceasefire headline. Macro theses can be correct on substance and still lose
  money on timing.
- **A single concentrated win does not validate the process.** The right
  question after a result like this isn't "did it work" but "would the
  process have generated this idea reliably, or did one trade carry the rest."
