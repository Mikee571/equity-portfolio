# 6. S&P 500 Benchmark

**Result (12 Apr - 14 May 2026): +14.0%**

## Thesis

The reference case: zero active management, zero strategy selection,
zero attempt to time the period's catalysts. Exists purely to answer one
question: did the effort spent on the other five strategies actually add
value relative to doing nothing?

## What happened

The index round-tripped through a steep, war-driven correction and then
staged a 14% rally on a ceasefire and a strong Nvidia earnings report,
ending the window essentially back near its starting trajectory.

## Why this matters more than its rank suggests

A pure index benchmark beat four of the five active strategies tested
(Statistical Arbitrage, 70/20/10, Volatility Arbitrage, Trend Following) and
only lost to Global Macro - and Global Macro's outperformance was
concentrated almost entirely in a single gold-miner position. That is an
uncomfortable but useful result: it is the honest baseline every other
strategy in this repository has to beat, and most of them, in this specific
window, didn't.

## Lessons learned

- **The benchmark is not a strawman - it is the bar.** Any active strategy
  that doesn't clear it after accounting for the extra risk and effort taken
  on has not earned its complexity.
- **A single 32-day window is not enough to declare a strategy "better" or
  "worse" than the index.** This result says something about this specific
  regime (a war shock followed by a relief rally), not about the
  strategies' expected value over a full market cycle.
