# 5. 70/20/10 Passive Portfolio

**Result (12 Apr – 14 May 2026): +8.2%**

## Thesis

A simple, low-maintenance diversified benchmark: 70% in a globally
diversified ETF as the core holding, 20% in individual value stocks selected
on fundamentals, and 10% in higher-risk/speculative exposure — designed to
survive difficult periods without active management rather than to maximize
returns in any single regime.

## Composition

| Sleeve | Weight | Holdings |
|---|---|---|
| Core | 70% | VWRL (global diversified ETF) |
| Value | 20% | XOM, RTX, JPM — selected on fundamentals (P/E, FCF, balance sheet strength) |
| Speculative | 10% | NVDA / Bitcoin — deliberately small, capped exposure |

## What happened

The diversified core (VWRL) cushioned the volatility of the war-driven
period better than any single-asset bet could, while the value sleeve —
JPM in particular — benefited from rate expectations. The speculative sleeve
was a minor drag around the Nvidia earnings event but, capped at 10%, never
threatened the portfolio's overall result.

## Lessons learned

- **Boring won by surviving, not by predicting anything correctly.** This
  strategy required no view on Hormuz, the Fed, or Nvidia's earnings — it
  simply stayed diversified enough that no single wrong call could do much
  damage.
- **The discipline rule mattered more than the allocation itself:** review
  monthly for rebalancing and quarterly for fundamentals, and otherwise leave
  it alone. The biggest risk to a passive strategy like this is not market
  risk — it's the temptation to fiddle with it during volatile weeks.
- **It is a sensible default, not a competitor for the top spot.** In a
  period with large, identifiable catalysts, concentrated and macro-aware
  strategies outperformed it. The value of 70/20/10 is as a floor — the
  strategy least likely to be the worst performer in any given regime — not
  as a ceiling.
