# 4. Trend Following

**Result (12 Apr – 14 May 2026): +2.7%**

## Thesis

Follow confirmed directional moves rather than predict reversals: ride
established trends in FX, commodities and rates where the underlying driver
(central bank divergence, structural demand, positioning) is expected to
persist over a multi-month horizon.

## Key positions

| Position | Direction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| USD/JPY | Long USD | Fed holding rates due to war-driven inflation vs. BoJ still accommodative — rate differential favors USD |
| Copper | Long | Structural multi-year demand story (AI data centers, electrification) against an inelastic supply pipeline (10–15 year mine permitting timelines) |
| 2Y/10Y Treasury spread | Steepener | Positioning for the curve to steepen once the Fed eventually cuts, based on a high-conviction desk call (cited: Morgan Stanley) |

## What happened

Both USD/JPY and copper produced small positive returns, broadly consistent
with the trend thesis, but the overall result was modest. The strategy
lagged the other five because trend-following systems are structurally
weak at capturing sharp regime reversals — and the dominant event of this
window was exactly that: an abrupt ceasefire that flipped market sentiment
from war-risk to relief-rally within days.

## Lessons learned

- **Trend following is the wrong tool for event-driven regime breaks.** The
  strategy was still positioned for the "war continues" regime when the
  market had already priced in peace. By construction, trend systems
  recognize a new regime only after it's been running for a while — they are
  reactive, not predictive, and that lag is the cost of the approach.
- **A multi-month horizon thesis (copper, rate differentials) needs a
  multi-month evaluation window.** Judging this strategy on a single 32-day
  snapshot understates its intended time horizon; the structural copper and
  curve-steepener theses are not meant to resolve this quickly.
- **The lowest-return strategy of the period is not necessarily the
  "worst" one.** It carried less idiosyncratic risk than the macro or
  volatility approaches and didn't require getting a binary event right —
  the cost of that lower risk was lower return in a period defined by sharp,
  fast-moving catalysts that favored more reactive strategies.
